# Middlehelm Wetlands Ruins

**Status:** planned (no hero mesh yet)  
**Camera:** first person, eye ~1.7 m  
**Grid:** 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 m  
**Primary ref:** Ruïne de l'Abbaye de Trois-Fontaines, Trois-Fontaines-l'Abbaye, Marne, Champagne-Ardenne

This brief is the art-direction and world pass. The machine contract is `brief.json`. Do not start a wall until this still feels true.

---

## Why this place

Trois-Fontaines is not “a cool ruin.” It is a Cistercian church that **failed as architecture**, not as a movie set.

Bernard’s first daughter-house (1118) was planted in wet woodland and *drained* so an austere house of God could stand. The church (c. 1160–1190) is Bernardine: pale Champagne limestone, cruciform, about 70 m long and 40 m across the transept. Pointed arcades. Pointed barrel vaults in the aisles. A west front of three large **round** arches under the ghost of a rose. Massive corbels still holding a few nave vaults. Other vaults came down as **bays**, taking the gutter walls, and in the 19th century they still lay almost whole among beeches that had grown in the nave.

That is the ruin grammar we steal:

- Geometry as devotion. Almost no grotesque.
- Bays as the unit of both construction and collapse.
- Trees and water occupying a plan that was meant to keep them out.
- The first three nave bays **wider** than the rest — modular, but not a stamp.

The 18th-century concave gateway, Corinthian pilasters, Louis XV pavilions are **not** this kit. That is a later occupation. Kill it.

Middlehelm is not Champagne. We translate: an austere order in the Middlehelm lowlands did the same founding act — drain a wetland forest at three springs, raise pale stone on a Bernardine plan. The wetlands came back. We push water further than the real park: standing water in the nave, reeds in the aisles, pile-rot at later timber shores. Overgrowth is **temperate**: beech, alder, willow, moss, ivy, reed. Not jungle.

## World (big)

**Who lived here.** A reforming order, Cistercian in all but name. They chose isolation. They farmed, milled, and kept the drains. The architecture is an argument: no narrative sculpture, no flamboyant tracery. Stone, proportion, light from the west triplet and the lost rose. Lay brothers in the west, choir monks east. The church was the dry place in a wet country.

**What happened.** The drains lost. Not a siege, not a dragon. Sluices silted. A mill pond backed up. Peat under the nave settled. Springs (the “three fountains”) reclaimed their basin. Water did not explode the church; it **occupied** it. Roof timber wicked and went first. Vaults failed the way they fail at Trois-Fontaines: a bay at a time, corbels still reaching into empty air, fallen vault fields lying as readable slabs. Beech and alder seeded the paving. Alder and willow took the flood margins. Reeds took the aisles where light and standing water meet. People stripped ashlar that was easy to reach; they left what was wet, heavy, or still vaulted. The west front still tries to be a church. The east is sky and water.

**Player path (FPS).** You do not overview this like Diablo. You **enter**. West portal (or a collapsed aisle wound) → narthex bay with no vault → three wider bays if we keep the Trois-Fontaines irregularity → arcade you can stand under → aisle as a wet corridor → crossing as a pool. Eye at 1.7 m: waterline, joint work, moss on the first course, the underside of a surviving vault, a tree trunk where a pier should be. At 12–20 m: the west wall still holding a rose fragment, the rhythm of pointed arches, a fallen bay as a landform.

**Scale.** Nave is a room you walk, not a canyon you survey. Standard bay **4 m**. Wide bay **8 m** (the real first-three-bays-wider fact, turned into a kit feature). Aisle clear **2 m**. Arcade pier **1 m**. Trim, step, waterline, pile spacing **0.5 m**. The original church at ~70 m is a layout target, not a requirement for the first assembly test. First socket-proof: 8 m of arcade + one aisle + one floor + waterline. Not a full abbey.

**Landmarks (must read as themselves).**

1. West front: three round-arched lights, rose remnant — the building still claiming to be a church.
2. A surviving vaulted bay on corbels next to an open bay — construction and failure in one look.
3. A beech (or alder) in the nave, socketed where paving failed.
4. Standing water occupying the crossing / low nave, with a **shared waterline** on every wall.

**Wrong for this place.**

- Generic mossy dungeon or crypt kit.
- Flamboyant Gothic, gargoyles, gold liturgical trim.
- 18th-century classical gate language from the real site.
- Tropical vines, jungle canopy, mangrove as default.
- Dry desert or high-mountain ruin.
- Isometric D4 chunk massing as the only silhouette (this is FPS; tactile at 0.5–2 m).
- Identical 4 m walls stamped down a nave with no wide-bay, no collapse-as-bay, no waterline.

## Art direction

**Silhouette.** Heavy, calm, vertical enough to be sacred, not spiky. Pointed arcade reads at distance; round west windows are the unexpected softness (Romanesque holding into early Gothic — keep that mix, it is Trois-Fontaines). Ruin silhouette is **missing vault + standing arcade**, not random spires. Trees break the skyline inside the plan, not as a forest wallpaper behind it.

**Material story.** One family: **pale Champagne-like limestone** (warm white to pink-grey). Wet and dry are the same stone in two states, not two materials pretending. Timber only as later shores, lost roof ghosts, pile remnants at foundations. Vegetation is overlay, not a second architecture. Metal is almost absent (a hinge, a grate) — Cistercian poverty.

**Wear, with causality.**

- **Waterline at 1.0 m** (high stain). **Standing water ~0.35 m** in the low nave. Every wall module shares this; it is the kit’s trim law.
- Algae and darkening on the first two courses; splash and capillary above.
- Joints opened by freeze and by roots, worse on the wet side.
- Vault soffits that still stand are cleaner (less weather) than the open wall-heads.
- Fallen bay slabs have their **weathered extrados up** or **clean soffit up** depending on how they landed — pick per piece, do not randomize into noise.
- Hand-height abrasion only where a surviving processional path is still used (west → crossing on higher paving). Most of this place is not a tourist loop; do not scuff everything.

**Modular without stamp.**

- 4 m standard bay + **8 m wide bay** (Trois-Fontaines nave irregularity).
- Collapse modules that still **socket** (a broken arcade that mates to a standing one).
- Overlay kit: ivy, root, reed, nave-tree — break the repeat without breaking the grid.
- Waterline trim continues through standing and broken walls so the flood is one event.
- Do not 45-degree the plan. Cistercian is orthogonal. Organic sits *in* the plan.

**How to use the ref.** Steal bay rhythm, arcade vs aisle, round west triplet, corbelled remaining vaults, fallen vaults as objects, trees in the nave, pale stone, austere capitals, the feeling that water and forest were invited back into a drained basin. Walk it in your head at 1.7 m.

**How not to copy.** Do not photogrammetry the Marne site. Do not include the 18th-century abbatial gate. Do not label it France. Middlehelm has its own order, its own three springs, its own failure. The ref is grammar, not a postcard.

## Kit as a language

**Flood law.** Standing water 0.35 m. Stain 1.0 m. Both on the 0.5 m grid. Pivots at floor grid, not at water.

**Sockets.** Wall-to-wall at 4 m and 8 m. Arcade to pier. Aisle to nave (2 m offset). Floor to wall. Cap. Inner/outer corner. Foundation pile to wall foot. Overlay sockets on wall faces (ivy) and floor (tree, reed). Fallen-vault pieces sit on floor sockets, not on a hidden 45.

**IDs (kit-level).**

| ID | Name | Use |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Ashlar dry | Above stain |
| 2 | Ashlar wet | Below stain, capillary |
| 3 | Core / rubble | Breaks, wall-heads, fallen fill |
| 4 | Vault soffit | Standing vault, collapsed slab soffit |
| 5 | Timber | Shores, plates, remnant ties |
| 6 | Vegetation | Overlays; keep as IDs not vertex-color-only |
| 7 | Metal | Rare fittings |

**UV.** Trim sheet for ashlar courses, waterline, and arcade profiles — that sheet is the kit. Unique UVs for west front, surviving vaulted bay, fallen bay slabs, nave tree. Stack modular walls. Texel locked across the family. UV2 only when a Source handoff is named; not blockout.

**On-grid vs not.** Walls, piers, floors, vaults, boardwalks: on 0.5–8 m. Roots, reed masses, broken rubble piles: may miss, but their **anchors** snap. If it cannot mate, it is not a module yet.

5. `MHWR_trim_waterline_1m` continuous

7. Player camera at 1.7 m through that vignette
